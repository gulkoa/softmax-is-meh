Conclusion
----------
At ALL q values tested, NR's autograd computes the correct
(implicit-function-theorem) Jacobian to machine precision, while BS's
autograd gives a different Jacobian — by O(1) at q=4 and q=16. This is
because BS's `torch.where(prob_sum > 0, mid, lb)` blocks gradients
through the λ computation, while NR's iterative `λ ← λ - f/f'` is
fully differentiable.

So even when forward outputs match to machine precision (e.g. q=4
where max|Δ|=1.2e-5 in fp32 / 2.78e-16 in fp64), training will see
substantially different gradients between the two implementations.
This is the mechanism behind the trained-accuracy OOD gap.
